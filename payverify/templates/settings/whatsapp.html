{{ icon('chat') }}Step-by-step setup
  1. Put {{ app_name }} online. WhatsApp sends each message to your server, so it needs a public https:// address. Run it on a small cloud server, or on this Mac with a tunnel such as ngrok. Set PUBLIC_BASE_URL to that address and BEHIND_PROXY=1, then restart (README → “Going live”).
  2. Create a Meta app. On developers.facebook.com → My Apps → Create app, choose the use case Connect with customers through WhatsApp (or the Business type), and link your Meta Business portfolio.
  3. Pick a number. In WhatsApp → API Setup you can start with Meta's free test number (add your own phone under “To” to receive messages), or add your business number. A number used here can't stay logged in to the normal WhatsApp app. Copy the Phone number ID into the form above.
  4. Create a permanent token. In business.facebook.com → Settings → Users → System users: add one with the Admin role, use Assign assets to give it your app and WhatsApp account, then Generate token with whatsapp_business_messaging and whatsapp_business_management. Paste it as the access token.
  5. Copy the app secret from App settings → Basic, paste it above, press Save, then Check connection.
  6. Connect the webhook. In WhatsApp → Configuration, press Edit next to Webhook, paste the Callback URL and Verify token from this page, and press Verify and save. Then, under Webhook fields, subscribe to messages.
  7. Test it. From your personal WhatsApp, send a payment screenshot to the business number. It shows up on the Payments screen within seconds, and you get the automatic reply.
  8. Go live. When you switch from the test number to your real number, change the Phone number ID here. Meta may ask you to verify your business before you can message many customers. Check Meta's WhatsApp pricing page for current message charges.
